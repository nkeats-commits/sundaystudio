Genesis 1 Commentary

This is navigation and chapter page chrome, not commentary.

1
In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.
The opening sentence presents creation as the deliberate work of God, not as the result of chance. The writer begins with the Creator before describing anything that was made. This order gives the chapter its theological center and teaches the reader to view every later distinction in the light of divine purpose.
2
And the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep.
The second observation describes the unfinished earth without treating its condition as a rival power. Darkness and the deep are named as parts of the created scene, while the Spirit of God is shown as present and active. The quiet movement from disorder toward form prepares the reader for the rhythm of the work that follows.
3
And God said, Let there be light: and there was light.
The light appears in response to the divine word. No struggle or instrument is required; the command itself is effective. This pattern is repeated throughout the chapter, so that each new feature of the world points back to the wisdom and authority of the One who speaks. The division of light from darkness also establishes a meaningful order for the days that follow.
4
And God saw the light, that it was good: and God divided the light from the darkness.
Having called the light into being, the Creator regards it with approval and assigns it a place within the ordered whole, showing that the goodness of creation is measured by its faithful relation to his purpose and that the separations made in this opening scene are not signs of hostility but are gracious boundaries that allow each gift to serve its appointed end while the unfolding account invites the reader to recognize in every movement from confusion to clarity the patient wisdom of God at work in the world and in human understanding.
